## Audience

Young Christians who already identify as believers and are inconsistent rather
than unconvinced. The content does not argue for faith; it assumes it and
challenges the gap between identity and behavior.

The CTA destination (`openyourbiblegirl.com`) is female-coded, which suggests the
audience skews female even though this is filed separately from the
self-improvement (women) niche.

## Diction & vocabulary

**We say:**

- Plain spoken English with contractions — "you can't", "you'll", "doesn't"
- Second person, always aimed at the viewer — "you", never "we" or "one"
- Short declarative sentences; fragments are used deliberately for rhythm
  ("The shows, the music, the scrolling.")
- Concrete enumeration instead of abstraction — idols become "your boyfriend or
  girlfriend, money, status, wealth"; content becomes "concerts, or the bar, or a
  party"
- Scripture by book, chapter, and verse, then **paraphrased** — never read out
  formally or quoted at length
- "claim to be a Christian" as the identity challenge in the hook. The 200k+
  script used it; the weaker delivered version softened to "if you're a
  Christian"

## Tone & posture

Convicting, not condemning. The speaker holds the viewer to a standard and then
puts herself under the same standard — *"and I was so guilty of this"* appears in
both seeded scripts, always late, always right before the CTA. That admission is
what keeps the callout from reading as superiority.

No greeting, no self-introduction, no warm-up. Both scripts open directly on the
claim.

## Hook conventions

The spoken hook states a count and an identity condition in one sentence, and
the on-screen text hook is written separately — see `hooks/christian.md`.

Scare quotes around "Christian" in the on-screen text do work the spoken line
can't: they signal the challenge visually before a word is said.

## CTA conventions

Observed in both scripts, in this order:

1. Tie every item back to one root cause — not being in scripture consistently
2. Name the struggle directly: "if consistency is something you struggle with"
3. Destination: `openyourbiblegirl.com`, and "text this number"
4. What the product does: "Folk will help remind you to be in scripture every
   single day"
5. Comment keyword: "Comment [WORD] and I'll send you the link"

The keyword varies between scripts ("BIBLE", "Folk"), so it is a per-script
choice rather than a fixed value.
